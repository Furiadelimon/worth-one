Screen time, by the hour

4 hours a day is 61 days a year.

If you scroll for about 4 hours every day, that is 1.67 years over the next ten years, and 3.33 years over twenty. Here is the receipt, line by line, and what the same time could be.

What 4 hours a day means in practice

4 hours of scrolling a day is 1,461 hours a year. Expressed in full 24-hour days it is 61 days, which is easier to feel: it is the length of a long holiday, spent in roughly 240-minute pieces you will not remember individually.

Over ten years the same habit is 14,610 hours. That is enough time to read about 2,435 books at six hours each, to learn 29 languages to a conversational level (roughly 500 hours each), or to watch every episode of Friends 167.9 times.

Compared with everyone else

The world average for social media is about 2 hours 20 minutes a day (DataReportal, Digital 2025). 4 hours a day is 72% above that average, which puts you in the "Heavy rotation" tier on the receipt. The tier names are a joke; the numbers are not.

The refund department

Cutting just 30 minutes a day from 4 hours gives back 7.6 days a year and 76 days over ten years, without changing anything else. Cutting it to one hour a day would return 45.7 days a year. None of this is advice; it is arithmetic you can act on or ignore.

How to check your real number

iPhone: Settings → Screen Time → See All App & Website Activity, weekly view, add up social and entertainment apps and divide by seven. Android: Settings → Digital Wellbeing → Dashboard. Then print your own receipt and send it to the friend who claims they "barely use their phone".
